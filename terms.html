7. Contributions and financial records

7.1 Recording actual contributions

A contribution recorded as an ordinary payment represents money that the Treasurer states has already been received by the group.

Ordinary contribution records are recorded as confirmed transactions. The Treasurer is responsible for entering the actual amount received and the applicable payment reference and allocation.

The Service is a record-management tool. Recording a payment in the Service does not itself prove that funds were transferred, settled or received by a financial institution.

7.2 Pledges

A pledge is an indicative statement of an amount a member expects or proposes to contribute. A pledge is not money received.

A pledge is not, merely because it is recorded in the Service, a debt, enforceable payment obligation, arrears balance or outstanding amount owed to the group.

Only an entry explicitly recorded as a pledge is treated as a pledge. Ordinary contribution recording does not create a pending payment.

7.3 Fulfilment of pledges

When the Treasurer receives actual money against a pledge, the existing pledge record may be converted into a confirmed payment record through the designated fulfilment process.

The actual amount received determines the contribution and transport allocation. It may be lower than, equal to or higher than the indicative pledged amount.

The original pledge reference and indicative pledge amount are retained as historical information. An actual payment reference is recorded separately where provided.

Unfulfilled pledges are excluded from calculations of money actually received, available funds, financial closing totals and the contributed indicator for an occasion.

7.4 Accuracy of financial information

Treasurers must verify the amounts, allocations, payment references and transaction details they enter. They must not knowingly record fictitious, duplicated or misleading financial transactions.

Any correction, reversal or adjustment must follow the permissions and financial controls provided by the Service.

8. Expenses and financial closing

Authorised Treasurers may record expenses and manage the financial closing of an occasion. Expense entries should accurately describe the amount, purpose and relevant details of the expenditure.

The financial closing process summarises confirmed money received and recorded expenses for the relevant occasion.

A finalised financial closing records a financial snapshot. Users must review the information carefully before confirming a closing.

A completed financial closing must not be treated as a guarantee that the underlying money is held by or has been independently verified by Prowess.
